\title{Anticipating demand in carbon-aware scheduling across data centres: guarantees and fair carbon attribution}

\author{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}

\begin{abstract}
Shifting deferrable jobs to cleaner hours and locations cuts data-centre emissions, but online schedulers must act before future grid conditions and jobs are known. This paper formulates multi-site, deadline-constrained scheduling of bag-of-tasks jobs as a minimum-cost flow problem and proposes CARMA (Carbon-aware Anticipatory Receding-horizon Multi-site Allocation). CARMA adds ``ghost jobs'' for expected arrivals to each hourly plan and uses a 1--24-hour carbon-intensity forecaster with adaptive conformal calibration. Three guarantees are proved. First, uncertainty about demand forces any online algorithm, even one with perfect carbon forecasts, to a competitive ratio of order $\sqrt{\theta}$, where $\theta$ is the ratio of the highest to the lowest unit emission; myopic planning is worse, at $(1+\theta)/2$. Second, given backstop capacity, CARMA never misses a deadline and is $\theta$-competitive under any predictions, is optimal under exact predictions, and degrades linearly with prediction error. Third, dual prices of the pooled problem yield a carbon attribution among sites that lies in the core, so no coalition gains by leaving. On three years of regional grid data for Great Britain, with 52 out-of-sample weeks in 2024, CARMA reduces emissions by 36--68\% relative to carbon-agnostic operation when migration is allowed. It comes within 5.8--8.7\% of the clairvoyant optimum, against 17.6--21.5\% for myopic control, and lowers late work. Anticipating demand is worth more than perfect carbon foresight, and CARMA degrades gracefully under injected errors. Location-based carbon accounting violates coalitional stability in every test week, whereas the dual attribution satisfies it exactly in hindsight and in over 99\% of checks online.
\end{abstract}
