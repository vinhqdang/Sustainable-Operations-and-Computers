\title{Anticipating demand in carbon-aware scheduling across data centres: guarantees and fair carbon attribution}

\author{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author. ORCID: 0000-0002-3877-8024}
\affiliation{organization={British University Vietnam},
            city={Hung Yen},

\begin{abstract}
Shifting deferrable jobs to cleaner hours and locations cuts data-centre emissions, but online schedulers act before future grid conditions and jobs are known. We formulate multi-site, deadline-constrained scheduling of bag-of-tasks jobs as a minimum-cost flow problem and propose CARMA (Carbon-aware Anticipatory Receding-horizon Multi-site Allocation), which adds ``ghost jobs'' for expected arrivals to each hourly plan. We prove that uncertainty about demand alone forces any online algorithm, even one with perfect carbon forecasts, to a competitive ratio of $\Omega(\sqrt{\theta})$, where $\theta$ is the ratio of the highest to the lowest unit emission, and myopic planning to at least $(1+\theta)/2$. Given backstop capacity, CARMA never misses a deadline under any predictions, is optimal under exact predictions and degrades linearly with prediction error. Dual prices of the pooled problem yield a carbon attribution among sites that lies in the core. On 2024 regional grid data for Great Britain, CARMA comes within 8.0--9.8\% of the clairvoyant optimum, against 15--16\% for the best myopic, protection-level or risk-averse baseline and 19--23\% for plain model-predictive control, and cuts attributional emissions by 36--68\% relative to carbon-agnostic operation. On a real cluster trace with a demand profile learned from different days, its gap is 20.7\% against 26.7--36.1\%. Anticipating demand is worth more than perfect carbon foresight. Under stylised marginal-emission accounting, achievable reductions shrink to 14\% and CARMA's edge becomes small, so physical savings depend on curtailed low-carbon supply. Origin- and host-based carbon accounting violate coalitional stability in every week, whereas the dual attribution never does in hindsight.
\end{abstract}
